Build your plate in thirds

Fruit & veg (fibre)Starchy carbs – wholegrain where you canProtein – beans, fish, eggs, meat, dairy

The NHS Eatwell Guide says fruit and veg should make up just over a third of what you eat, and starchy foods just over a third, with protein, dairy and a little oil making up the rest. Tick the three parts on each meal on the Today screen to see how balanced your plates are.

You don't need this balance at every meal – aim for it across a day or even a week.

The Eatwell Guide – NHS

5 A Day

  • One portion is 80g of fresh, frozen or tinned fruit or veg – e.g. 1 apple or banana, 3 heaped tablespoons of cooked veg, or 7 cherry tomatoes.
  • 30g of dried fruit counts as 1 – best at mealtimes for your teeth.
  • Beans and pulses count once a day, however much you have.
  • Juice and smoothies: no more than 150ml a day in total, and they count once.
  • Potatoes don't count – they're a starchy food.
5 A Day portion sizes – NHS

Starchy foods & fibre

  • Base meals on potatoes, bread, rice or pasta, choosing wholegrain or higher-fibre versions.
  • Leave the skins on potatoes for extra fibre.
  • Adults should aim for 30g of fibre a day – oats, wholegrains, beans, lentils, fruit and veg all help.

Protein

  • Mix it up: beans, pulses, fish, eggs, meat and dairy.
  • Aim for 2 portions of fish a week, one of them oily, like salmon or mackerel.
  • Choose lean cuts and eat less red and processed meat.
  • If you train, spread protein across your meals instead of saving it for one.

Keep these low

  • Saturated fat: no more than 30g a day for men and 20g for women.
  • Salt: no more than 6g a day (about a teaspoon).
  • Free sugars: no more than 30g a day. These are sugars added to food, plus honey, syrups and fruit juice. Labels don't separate them out, so check the ingredients.
  • On labels, more than 22.5g of sugar per 100g is high, and 5g or less is low.
8 tips for healthy eating – NHS

Drinks

The NHS general guide is 6 to 8 glasses of fluid a day. Your personal target on the Targets tab is based on your weight instead: 30–35ml per kg, and at least 2 litres for men or 1.6 litres for women.

Water, lower-fat milk and sugar-free drinks, including tea and coffee, all count. Drink more on training days or when it's hot. Pale straw-coloured pee is a good sign you're drinking enough.

Water, drinks and hydration – NHS

A healthy relationship with tracking

Tracking is a tool for learning what's in your food. It isn't a test you can fail.

  • Look at weekly averages instead of any single day.
  • Take days off – birthdays, meals out and holidays don't need logging.
  • Turn off calorie numbers (on the Targets tab) if they start to take over.

If tracking starts to make you anxious, or food feels like it's controlling you, take a break and talk to someone. Beat, the UK eating disorder charity, runs a free helpline: 0808 801 0677.

Weigh-ins

How these are worked out

Calories: Mifflin-St Jeor resting rate × activity, then the goal surplus or deficit.
Protein: your chosen g/kg. Benefit levels off around 1.6 g/kg; 1.6–2.2 g/kg is the usual range for building muscle.
Fat: 1 g/kg (common range 0.5–1.5 g/kg).
Carbs: the rest of your calories. For training you want at least 3–5 g/kg.
Fibre: 30g a day, the UK adult guideline.
Water: 30–35ml per kg of body weight, a rule dietitians commonly use. It never goes below the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) figure for drinks: 2 litres for men and 1.6 litres for women. Height isn't used because it doesn't add anything once weight is known. Drink more on training days and in hot weather.
